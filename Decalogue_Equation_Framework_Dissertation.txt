THE DECALOGUE EQUATION FRAMEWORK
A DISSERTATION PLAN FOR THE TEN MATHEMATICAL COMMANDMENTS

Working subtitle:
An Axiomatic and Computational System for Auditing Mathematical Derivations

Authors:
Stacey Szmy, ChatGPT, Gemini AI, and AI analytic collaborators


======================================================================
ABSTRACT
======================================================================

The Decalogue Equation Framework is a proposed mathematical auditing system
inspired by the structural form of the Ten Commandments. Its purpose is not
to assign religious truth values to mathematics, but to use the metaphor of
commandments, judgment, error, repair, and final verdict as a memorable formal
language for evaluating mathematical derivations.

The framework translates ten moral imperatives into ten mathematical audit
principles governing foundations, symbol definition, completion criteria,
derivational lineage, information preservation, domain compatibility,
algebraic accounting, logical truth, variable binding, and lawful use of
assumptions and bounds.

A mathematical object is represented as

    E = (D, Sigma, A, T, x_0),

where:

    D       is the domain,
    Sigma   is the collection of symbols and operators,
    A       is the declared assumption set,
    T       is the ordered sequence of transformations,
    x_0     is the initial expression or state.

Each commandment produces an audit result:

    C_i(E) in {PASS, FAIL, UNDETERMINED}.

The framework then assigns a Grace Score and a final classification:

    HEAVEN      all commandments pass;
    PURGATORY   one or more repairable failures remain;
    LIMBO       no failure is established, but information is insufficient;
    HELL        an unresolved fatal contradiction or undefined operation exists.

The central principle is:

    Heaven is not convergence. Heaven is lawful derivation.
    Hell is not infinity. Hell is unresolved contradiction.

The dissertation will formalize the framework, distinguish logical validity
from numerical behavior, establish the audit predicates, present worked
examples, and define a Python implementation called the Decalogue Equation
Auditor.


======================================================================
1. INTRODUCTION
======================================================================

1.1 Motivation

Mathematical errors often arise not from difficult calculations but from
violations of basic structural obligations:

    - a domain is changed without notice;
    - a symbol is used before definition;
    - a branch of a solution is discarded;
    - an implication is reversed;
    - an assumption is quietly strengthened;
    - an approximation is presented as equality;
    - an operation is applied outside its lawful domain.

These failures are often taught separately. The Decalogue Equation Framework
collects them into a single memorable audit architecture.

The project uses the Ten Commandments as a symbolic template for ten rules of
mathematical conduct. The metaphor provides names and narrative structure,
while the actual system remains secular, formal, and testable.

1.2 Main research question

Can a broad class of mathematical derivations be audited through a finite set
of explicit structural rules that identify invalid steps, distinguish fatal
errors from repairable omissions, and generate a transparent final verdict?

1.3 Supporting questions

    1. What mathematical obligations should correspond to each commandment?
    2. Can the rules be represented as formal predicates?
    3. Which rules can be checked automatically?
    4. Which rules require human judgment or supplied theorem certificates?
    5. How should uncertainty be represented?
    6. How should repairable and fatal violations be distinguished?
    7. Can a weighted score summarize an audit without replacing the detailed
       logical report?
    8. How should the framework handle divergent, chaotic, non-injective, or
       approximate mathematics that is nevertheless valid?

1.4 Central correction to the original concept

Divergence, chaos, infinity, non-injectivity, and information loss are not
automatically mathematical failures.

For example:

    f(n) = n^2

diverges as n approaches infinity, but the expression is lawful.

Likewise, a constant map may destroy distinctions between inputs while still
being a valid function.

Therefore:

    HEAVEN must mean lawful derivation, not boundedness.
    HELL must mean unresolved contradiction, undefinedness, or invalidity,
    not merely divergence or complexity.

2. FOUNDATIONAL MODEL
======================================================================

2.1 Mathematical object

Let a derivation object be

    E = (D, Sigma, A, T, x_0, G),

where:

    D       is the declared domain;
    Sigma   is the symbol and operator table;
    A       is the set of assumptions;
    T       is the sequence of transformations;
    x_0     is the initial state;
    G       is the stated goal or conclusion.

A derivation may be written as

    E_0 -> E_1 -> E_2 -> ... -> E_n,

with a justification certificate J_k attached to every transition:

    E_k --J_k--> E_(k+1).

2.2 Audit states

Each commandment returns one of three values:

    PASS
    FAIL
    UNDETERMINED

Numerically, these may be encoded as

    PASS            = 1
    UNDETERMINED    = 1/2
    FAIL            = 0

This three-state design prevents the auditor from confusing the absence of a
detected error with proof of correctness.

2.3 Violation vector

Define

    V(E) = (v_1, v_2, ..., v_10),

where

    v_i = 0          if commandment i passes,
    v_i = 1/2        if commandment i is undetermined,
    v_i = 1          if commandment i fails.

A simpler binary violation count may also be used when all rules are decidable:

    N_sin(E) = sum(v_i),

with v_i in {0,1}.

The term "sin" is metaphorical and comedic. Mathematically it is only a count
of detected violations.

2.4 Grace Score

Assign weights w_i such that

    w_i > 0
    and
    sum(w_i) = 1.

Let s_i be the audit score for commandment i:

    s_i = 1          for PASS,
    s_i = 1/2        for UNDETERMINED,
    s_i = 0          for FAIL.

Then define

    Grace(E) = sum(w_i * s_i).

Thus,

    0 <= Grace(E) <= 1.

The score is diagnostic only. It must never override a fatal contradiction.


======================================================================
3. THE TEN MATHEMATICAL COMMANDMENTS
======================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMANDMENT I
THE COMMANDMENT OF FOUNDATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Original inspiration:

    You shall have no strange gods before me.

Mathematical principle:

    A mathematical system must declare a coherent foundational structure.

Audit requirement:

    The domain, equality relation, constants, axioms, and governing operations
    must be mutually compatible.

Pass condition:

    C_1(E) = PASS

when all expressions and transformations remain within the declared or
explicitly extended foundational setting.

Typical violation:

    x in R
    followed by
    x^2 = -1

without extending the system from the real numbers R to the complex numbers C.

Interpretation:

The commandment does not forbid multiple mathematical structures. It forbids
silent competition between incompatible structures. Multiple domains are lawful
only when the maps between them are declared.

Research task:

Define a formal foundation-consistency checker based on domains, assumptions,
and operator signatures.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMANDMENT II
THE COMMANDMENT OF MEANING
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Original inspiration:

    You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain.

Mathematical principle:

    Every symbol must be defined and used consistently.

Formal condition:

    FreeSymbols(E) is a subset of DefinedSymbols(E).

Audit targets:

    - undefined variables;
    - symbols whose meaning changes mid-derivation;
    - undefined functions;
    - undefined operators;
    - physical or mathematical terminology used without definition;
    - expressions with no declared semantic role.

Typical violations:

    - using k without defining k;
    - treating f first as a function and later as a scalar;
    - invoking "entropy" without specifying which entropy;
    - writing 0/0 as though it were a number.

Interpretation:

This is the semantic integrity commandment. It rejects empty symbolism and
ambiguous notation.

Research task:

Construct a symbol table and semantic consistency protocol.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMANDMENT III
THE COMMANDMENT OF REST AND COMPLETION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Original inspiration:

    Remember to keep holy the Lord's Day.

Mathematical principle:

    Every iterative, recursive, or procedural argument must declare how its
    progress, completion, or limiting behavior is evaluated.

For an iteration

    x_(n+1) = T(x_n),

at least one of the following should be supplied:

    n = N
    ||x_(n+1) - x_n|| < epsilon
    x_n -> L
    I(x_(n+1)) = I(x_n)
    a cycle or recurrence condition
    an externally declared stopping rule

This commandment does not require all processes to converge or repeat.
It requires the process to state what counts as completion, rest, checkpoint,
or lawful continuation.

Typical violation:

An infinite iterative scheme is presented without a stopping condition,
invariant, convergence claim, divergence claim, or interpretation.

Research task:

Develop a completion-schema library for finite, iterative, recursive, and
asymptotic derivations.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMANDMENT IV
THE COMMANDMENT OF LINEAGE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Original inspiration:

    Honor your father and your mother.

Mathematical principle:

    Every derived expression must descend from its parent expressions through
    a valid and identifiable transformation.

For

    E_0 -> E_1 -> ... -> E_n,

every step must have a certificate:

    E_k --J_k--> E_(k+1).

Possible certificates include:

    - substitution;
    - distributivity;
    - factorization;
    - theorem application;
    - differentiation;
    - integration;
    - logical implication;
    - modular reduction;
    - approximation with stated error;
    - numerical evaluation;
    - change of variables.

A conclusion with no derivational ancestry is called an orphan expression.

Typical violation:

A result appears after several lines with no operation explaining how it was
obtained.

Research task:

Define a machine-readable proof-step format and justification ledger.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMANDMENT V
THE COMMANDMENT OF PRESERVATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Original inspiration:

    You shall not kill.

Mathematical principle:

    A transformation must not silently destroy mathematically relevant
    information.

If

    T: D -> R

is non-injective, the information loss must be acknowledged through fibers,
equivalence classes, branches, kernels, discarded terms, or error bounds.

For example:

    x^2 = 4
    therefore
    x = 2

is incomplete because x = -2 has been silently eliminated.

The lawful conclusion is

    x = +/-2.

Information loss is permitted when declared. Examples include:

    - rounding;
    - projection;
    - averaging;
    - quotienting;
    - lossy compression;
    - truncation;
    - branch selection under an assumption;
    - ignoring higher-order terms with an error estimate.

Typical violation:

Cancellation, squaring, rooting, projection, or approximation removes possible
solutions or distinctions without recording the loss.

Research task:

Classify information-losing operations and define preservation warnings.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMANDMENT VI
THE COMMANDMENT OF COMPATIBILITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Original inspiration:

    You shall not commit adultery.

Mathematical principle:

    Objects from different domains may interact only through a declared and
    lawful bridge.

If

    x in D_1
    and
    y in D_2,

then combining them requires a map such as

    phi: D_1 -> D_2

or an operation

    B: D_1 x D_2 -> D_3.

Typical violations:

    - treating modular equality as ordinary equality;
    - adding a vector to a probability distribution without definition;
    - mixing units without dimensional conversion;
    - applying a real logarithm to a negative number without extending to C;
    - combining discrete and continuous models without an interface rule.

Interpretation:

The rule does not demand absolute separation. It demands lawful coupling.

Research task:

Create a domain-and-type compatibility checker with explicit coercion maps.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMANDMENT VII
THE COMMANDMENT OF ACCOUNTING
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Original inspiration:

    You shall not steal.

Mathematical principle:

    No quantity may enter or leave a derivation without being accounted for.

For an equation

    L_k = R_k,

a transformation to

    L_(k+1) = R_(k+1)

must apply lawful operations consistently, subject to domain restrictions.

Example of violation:

    x + 3 = 7
    therefore
    x = 7.

The 3 has been stolen from the derivation.

Lawful transformation:

    x + 3 - 3 = 7 - 3
    therefore
    x = 4.

Applications include:

    - algebraic balance;
    - conservation of probability;
    - conservation of mass, energy, charge, or momentum when assumed;
    - coefficient accounting;
    - term-by-term transformation;
    - normalization.

Research task:

Design an equation-balance and conservation ledger.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMANDMENT VIII
THE COMMANDMENT OF TRUTH
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Original inspiration:

    You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor.

Mathematical principle:

    Every equality, approximation, implication, and equivalence symbol must
    express the correct logical relationship.

The framework distinguishes:

    =
    approximately equal
    implies
    is implied by
    if and only if
    proportional to
    asymptotic to

Example:

    x^2 = 1 implies x = 1

is false over R.

However,

    x = 1 implies x^2 = 1

is true.

This commandment detects:

    - false equivalence;
    - reversed implication;
    - unsupported theorem claims;
    - empirical evidence presented as proof;
    - approximation presented as exact equality;
    - local validity presented as global validity.

Research task:

Formalize a relation-type checker and symbolic equivalence tester.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMANDMENT IX
THE COMMANDMENT OF LEGITIMATE BINDING
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Original inspiration:

    You shall not covet your neighbor's wife.

Mathematical principle:

    Variables, indices, functions, parameters, and quantifiers must retain
    their lawful identities and scopes.

For example,

    for every x in D, there exists y in D such that P(x,y)

is not generally equivalent to

    there exists y in D such that for every x in D, P(x,y).

Quantifier order matters.

Other violations include:

    - reusing n as both a fixed constant and a running index;
    - accidental variable capture inside a summation or integral;
    - replacing one parameter with another because they have similar names;
    - assuming independence because variables are visually distinct;
    - binding a free variable without declaration.

Research task:

Implement variable-scope, index, and quantifier tracking.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMANDMENT X
THE COMMANDMENT OF OWNERSHIP AND BOUNDS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Original inspiration:

    You shall not covet your neighbor's goods.

Mathematical principle:

    A derivation may use only the assumptions, data, resources, domains, and
    bounds legitimately available to it.

If a theorem assumes

    x in [0,1],

the proof may not quietly use x > 1.

If a statistical model is trained on sample S, it may not claim certainty over
an unrelated population without a generalization argument.

The rule also covers rearrangement of infinite series. A conditionally
convergent series may not be rearranged as though absolute convergence had
already been established.

Typical violations:

    - importing an unstated assumption;
    - borrowing a theorem whose hypotheses are unmet;
    - extending finite evidence to an infinite claim;
    - using stronger bounds than those proved;
    - claiming data not present in the sample.

Research task:

Build an assumption and bound provenance ledger.


======================================================================
4. VERDICT SYSTEM
======================================================================

4.1 Strict verdict

Define the final verdict by

    HEAVEN

when every commandment passes.

    PURGATORY

when one or more failures exist but all are identified and repairable.

    LIMBO

when no fatal failure has been proved, but one or more commandments remain
undetermined because the available information is insufficient.

    HELL

when an unresolved fatal contradiction, undefined operation, impossible domain
claim, or knowingly invalid transformation remains in the derivation.

4.2 Mortal violations

Some failures should block a HEAVEN verdict regardless of the weighted score.

Candidate mortal violations include:

    - foundational contradiction;
    - undefined central symbols or operations;
    - false equality or false equivalence;
    - division by zero;
    - theorem use with violated hypotheses;
    - contradiction between assumptions;
    - invalid domain transition.

Define a fatality indicator:

    M(E) = 1

when at least one unresolved mortal violation exists.

Then

    M(E) = 1
    implies
    Verdict(E) is not HEAVEN.

4.3 Priority of logic over score

A high Grace Score cannot rescue a fatal contradiction.

For example, an equation may pass nine commandments and fail Truth through a
false equality. The score may be 0.9, but the final verdict cannot be HEAVEN.

The score summarizes quality. The verdict enforces validity.


======================================================================
5. WORKED EXAMPLE
======================================================================

Consider

    (x^2 - 1)/(x - 1) = x + 1.

Factorization gives

    ((x - 1)(x + 1))/(x - 1) = x + 1.

The cancellation is valid only when

    x != 1.

Therefore the correct statement is

    (x^2 - 1)/(x - 1) = x + 1,
    for x in R \ {1}.

Without the restriction x != 1, the unrestricted equation falsely suggests
that the left side is defined at x = 1.

Possible initial audit:

    I.   Foundation                    PASS
    II.  Meaning                       PASS
    III. Rest and Completion           PASS
    IV.  Lineage                       PASS
    V.   Preservation                  FAIL
    VI.  Compatibility                 PASS
    VII. Accounting                    PASS
    VIII.Truth                         FAIL
    IX.  Legitimate Binding            PASS
    X.   Ownership and Bounds          FAIL

Reason:

    The excluded domain point x = 1 was erased, the equality was stated too
    broadly, and the derivation used cancellation outside its lawful bound.

After adding x != 1:

    all commandments                   PASS
    Grace Score                        1
    Verdict                            HEAVEN

7. SOFTWARE PLAN
======================================================================

Proposed filename:

    DECALOGUE_EQUATION_AUDITOR.py

Core modules:

    1. Input Parser
       Reads domains, symbols, assumptions, goals, expressions, and
       justifications.

    2. Symbol Registry
       Tracks definitions, types, scopes, and semantic changes.

    3. Domain Engine
       Checks whether operations are valid over the declared domains.

    4. Assumption Ledger
       Records every assumption and tracks where it is used.

    5. Transformation Auditor
       Tests symbolic equivalence or implication between consecutive steps.

    6. Information-Loss Detector
       Warns about squaring, rooting, cancellation, projection, rounding, and
       branch removal.

    7. Binding Inspector
       Tracks free variables, bound variables, indices, and quantifiers.

    8. Completion Inspector
       Checks for goals, stopping conditions, limits, or invariants.

    9. Grace Score Engine
       Computes weighted commandment scores.

    10. Verdict Engine
        Produces Heaven, Purgatory, Limbo, or Hell.

    11. Report Generator
        Produces a detailed commandment-by-commandment audit.

Example input format:

    DOMAIN: real
    DEFINED: x
    ASSUME: x != 1
    GOAL: simplify expression

    STEP 1: (x^2 - 1)/(x - 1)
    STEP 2: ((x - 1)*(x + 1))/(x - 1)
    JUSTIFICATION 1->2: difference of squares

    STEP 3: x + 1
    JUSTIFICATION 2->3: cancel x - 1

Example output format:

    I.   Foundation                    PASS
    II.  Meaning                       PASS
    III. Rest and Completion           PASS
    IV.  Lineage                       PASS
    V.   Preservation                  PASS
    VI.  Compatibility                 PASS
    VII. Accounting                    PASS
    VIII.Truth                         PASS
    IX.  Legitimate Binding            PASS
    X.   Ownership and Bounds          PASS

    Grace Score: 1.000
    Mortal Violations: 0
    Verdict: HEAVEN

8. RESEARCH LIMITATIONS
======================================================================

The framework must openly acknowledge the following limits:

    1. No general computer program can automatically determine the truth of all
       mathematical statements.

    2. Some theorem applications require external proof libraries or human
       verification.

    3. Natural-language mathematics may be too ambiguous for exact parsing.

    4. A symbolic equivalence test may fail even when two expressions are
       mathematically equivalent.

    5. Numerical agreement does not prove symbolic equivalence.

    6. The Grace Score is not a proof.

    7. The religious terminology is metaphorical and must not be presented as
       theological judgment.

    8. Different branches of mathematics may require specialized versions of
       the commandments.

These limitations are not weaknesses to hide. They define the lawful scope of
the project under its own Tenth Commandment.

======================================================================
11. WORKING THESIS STATEMENT
======================================================================

A mathematical derivation can be usefully evaluated as a structured object
subject to ten explicit obligations concerning foundation, meaning, completion,
lineage, preservation, compatibility, accounting, truth, binding, and bounds.
Although no finite audit system can decide all mathematics, a commandment-based
framework can identify broad classes of invalid reasoning, distinguish
repairable omissions from fatal contradictions, and improve the transparency
of both human and machine-generated mathematical arguments.


======================================================================
12. CLOSING FORMULATION
======================================================================

The Decalogue Equation Framework does not ask whether an equation is simple,
beautiful, finite, convergent, or predictable.

It asks:

    Was the domain declared?
    Were the symbols defined?
    Was the process completed or interpreted?
    Did every result descend lawfully from its parents?
    Was information silently destroyed?
    Were incompatible objects mixed without a bridge?
    Was every mathematical quantity accounted for?
    Were the logical relations truthful?
    Were variables and quantifiers bound lawfully?
    Were assumptions, bounds, and resources used honestly?

The final maxim of the framework is:

    Heaven is not convergence.
    Heaven is lawful derivation.

    Hell is not infinity.
    Hell is unresolved contradiction.
